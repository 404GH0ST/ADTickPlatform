## Anti-Patterns Verdict

**LLM assessment:** Pass. The frontend does not immediately look AI-generated. It avoids gradients, glass, neon, oversized hero metrics, excessive rounding, and decorative cybersecurity theater. IBM Plex Sans, sharp rules, compact controls, summary strips, and textual status cues create a credible operational system. The risk is “generated enterprise completeness”: almost every capability receives a card, description, metadata, and actions. On `/admin/game` and attack views, that repeated grammar becomes uniform enough to feel assembled rather than directed. Four schemes plus three modes also feels product-demo generous for a mission-critical tool.

**Deterministic scan:** One warning across 74 scanned frontend files: `layout-transition` at `apps/web/app/globals.css:709` (`transition: max-width`). The finding applies to the expanding appearance controls; related option styles also transition `max-width` and padding. It is not a false positive, although practical severity is low because the control is small, runs for 220ms, and reduced-motion overrides exist.

**Visual overlays:** No reliable user-visible overlay is available. Browser automation/mutable injection was unavailable to the detector assessment. The independent design assessment started the mock API and Next production server, but Chromium failed before navigation because the environment sandbox denied launch. Source inspection and the deterministic scan are the fallback evidence.

## Overall Impression

This is a coherent, trustworthy operational UI with unusually strong status communication. Its biggest opportunity is not cosmetic: the interface must become more decisive under pressure. Current pages often expose every valid capability at once, forcing organizers and participants to build their own hierarchy while the event is live.

## What's Working

1. **Coherent visual system.** Graphite-first tokens, sharp radii, compact spacing, IBM Plex Sans, semantic tones, and consistent controls make participant and organizer surfaces feel like one product.
2. **Excellent status communication.** Match lifecycle, API degradation, scoreboard freeze, countdowns, readiness, filtered/live state, and pending operations use text as well as color.
3. **Real information architecture effort.** The organizer game page puts incident response before routine controls, observation afterward, and checker diagnostics behind progressive disclosure.

## Priority Issues

### [P1] High-risk organizer actions lack a phase-aware command hierarchy

**Why it matters:** Start, Pause, Resume, Stop, Advance Tick, Recompute, and Audit appear as adjacent compact controls with similar weight. During an incident, an operator must parse the whole control band and can choose the wrong intervention.

**Fix:** Make current match state and one safe next action dominant. Move refresh into utility chrome; separate lifecycle, destructive, and repair actions; add explicit consequence copy and confirmation where Stop, Advance Tick, or recomputation can materially change event state.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable harden apps/web/app/admin/game`

### [P1] Attack investigation exposes query mechanics instead of tactical questions

**Why it matters:** Seven filter inputs—including limit and offset—plus paging, counts, map, summary, and table create more than four simultaneous decisions before insight. Users want answers such as “who hit us, on which service, during which ticks?”

**Fix:** Lead with presets such as Against us, By us, Current tick, and Last 5 ticks. Put advanced filters behind disclosure, replace offset with conventional pagination, show applied filters clearly, and allow saved views.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable distill apps/web/app/attacks`

### [P1] Navigation and shell chrome are overexposed

**Why it matters:** Nine organizer destinations and a participant header containing navigation, appearance, VPN, manual, account, logout, and readiness give low-frequency utilities equal presence with the current task.

**Fix:** Group organizer routes into Run Event, Configure, People, and Evidence. Keep four or five primary destinations and move API, audit, and deployment utilities into secondary navigation. Move appearance into account/preferences and show VPN/readiness only when actionable.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable shape navigation and shell information architecture`

### [P2] Empty states often diagnose absence without teaching recovery

**Why it matters:** States such as no owned services, no accepted attacks, and no scheduler events do not consistently tell users whether to wait, reset filters, join a team, refresh, or contact an organizer.

**Fix:** Make empty states conditional: identify the likely cause, provide the relevant action, and state whether data will populate automatically.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable onboard empty and degraded states`

### [P2] Repeated card grammar flattens hierarchy on dense pages

**Why it matters:** Card title, description, bordered content, and action row repeat across nearly every concept. Important live state and low-frequency configuration therefore read too similarly, especially on `/admin/game`.

**Fix:** Reserve cards for independently actionable modules. Use section-level tables, inline status strips, and subdued disclosures for supporting information; remove explanatory prose where structure and labels already communicate purpose.

**Suggested command:** `$impeccable layout dense organizer and attack pages`

## Persona Red Flags

**Rina, live-event organizer under incident pressure:** On `/admin/game`, Incident Response, Match Controls, Match Observation, Scheduler, and Checker Investigation remain visible while many refresh and lifecycle actions compete. “Trusted runtime truth” explains architecture instead of the safest intervention. Stop, recompute, and advance controls need stronger consequence separation.

**Dimas, experienced participant switching between exploit and defense:** The shell repeatedly consumes attention with appearance, manual, VPN, account, readiness, summaries, alerts, and navigation. On Attacks, he cannot jump directly to attacks against his team this tick; he must rebuild filters and understand pagination mechanics.

**Nadia, first-time participant:** Overview actions help orientation, but Owned Services, Enemy Targets, unlock proof, live head, SLA, and API Base assume event-model knowledge. Empty states do not consistently distinguish no team membership, match not started, filtered-out results, or actual absence.

## Minor Observations

- Validate the 11px uppercase summary labels across all eight scheme/mode combinations and lower-quality event laptops.
- Four complete schemes dilute Graphite’s product recognition; Paper is distinctive but may not justify equal prominence.
- Hover/focus expansion for appearance is an invented affordance; a standard popover would be more discoverable and avoid the detector’s layout-transition warning.
- Loading spinners are common. Stable button labels plus adjacent progress text would reduce recognition and layout shifts.
- Login is appropriately quiet but could show event identity so users can verify the competition before submitting credentials.
- The map should earn its vertical space by answering a distinct tactical question; otherwise it belongs as a secondary view.

## Questions to Consider

- What if `/admin/game` showed only the safe next action for the current phase and placed exceptional interventions behind Incident tools?
- Are Ink, Paper, and Moss helping operators, or showcasing the design system at the expense of recognizable product identity?
- If the attack map disappeared, which decision would become harder? If none, should it be secondary rather than part of the default investigation stack?
- Could the participant’s team, current tick, and threatened services become the default lens everywhere?
- What should create confidence after Match finished: a frozen snapshot, exported evidence, verified scoring audit, or handoff checklist?
